The short version

Your data lives on your phone. There is no account, and we never ask for your name, email or a password.

Two AI features send some of it away for a few seconds, and only when you use them. The AI photo estimate sends your photos with your sex, age, height and weight. The weekly read (Pro) sends a summary of your numbers: your trend weight, your recent body fat results and a few measurements. Both go to our server and on to OpenAI to produce an answer, and are then discarded. They are never saved on our servers and never used to train anything.

Free users see ads, which are not personalised. Subscriptions are handled by Google Play. The rest of this page spells it out.

Photos and the AI estimate

Your photos never leave your phone unless you tap the AI estimate button, and then only for the seconds it takes to get an answer.

When you request an AI estimate, this is exactly what happens:

  1. The app shrinks the photo (or up to three photos: front, side, back) to about 1,000 pixels on the long side.
  2. It sends them to our server over an encrypted connection, along with your sex, age, height and weight.
  3. Our server passes them straight to OpenAI to produce the estimate.
  4. The answer comes back as text: a number, a range, and a few words about what was visible.
  5. Our server returns that text to your phone and discards the photos. They are not written to disk, a database or a log.

The original photos stay in the app's private folder on your phone so you can see them in your history. You can delete any of them at any time from the session screen.

OpenAI processes the photos only to produce the estimate. Under its API terms it does not use them to train its models, and may retain API inputs for up to 30 days for abuse monitoring before deleting them.

The estimate is an opinion from an AI, not a medical measurement. It can be wrong. Please don't use it to diagnose or treat anything.

The weekly read

The weekly read is a Pro feature. Nothing is sent until you tap Get my read (or tap the weekly notification, which opens the app and asks for it). Then the app sends our server, over an encrypted connection:

  • your sex, age, height, and your goal weight and goal body fat if you set them;
  • your trend weight now, a week ago and a month ago, your weekly change, and how many days you weighed in over the last 7 and 30 days;
  • your last six body fat sessions: the date, the result and its range, each method's number, any note where two methods disagreed, your waist, and one caliper fold.

Our server checks that data, passes it to OpenAI to write the note, returns the note to your phone, and discards the data. It is not written to disk, a database or a log. No photos, names or dates of birth are sent. OpenAI's terms are the same as for photos above.

Subscriptions and identifiers

There is no login. Two identifiers do the work an account would normally do, and neither is tied to your name.

Anonymous ID. The first time you open the app it asks our server for a random ID and stores it on your phone. It lets our server count how many AI estimates you have used this month. If you uninstall the app, your phone forgets it. Our hosting provider keeps the sign-in record for that ID until you ask us to delete it (see Delete what is on our server, below).

Device ID. Android gives each app a stable ID for your device. The app sends it with AI photo requests and promo codes. We keep it with the one free AI estimate, so that reinstalling the app doesn't grant another one, and with one-phone promo codes. It is not your advertising ID, and it is not used for advertising or for tracking you across apps.

Google Play billing. If you subscribe, payment is handled entirely by Google Play using the Google account on your phone. We never see your card, your name or your email. We use RevenueCat to check whether a subscription is active; it receives your anonymous ID and the purchase details Google Play shares with it, nothing else.

AI usage log. Our server keeps a row per AI request: your anonymous ID, the subscription it belongs to, the kind of request, the time, the AI prompt version, and whether the request was refunded; for the free estimate, also your device ID. This is how the monthly and daily limits work. It contains no photos, no measurements and no results. Rows are deleted after about 13 months.

Request log. To stop any one install overloading a feature, our server also notes your anonymous ID, which feature you used and the time. These rows are deleted within 6 hours.

Backup, export and deleting your data

Because your data lives on your phone, you are in charge of it.

  • Delete anything. Swipe a weight entry or open a session and tap Delete. Photos are removed from the phone at the same time.
  • Delete everything. Uninstall the app. All data and photos in the app's private folder go with it. Our server keeps only the records described above: AI usage rows (deleted after about 13 months), the sign-in record for your anonymous ID, and any promo-code records. They hold no name or contact details. To remove them now, use Delete my data.
  • Delete what is on our server. In Settings, under Your data, tap Delete my data. It opens your email app with your anonymous ID and device ID filled in. Send it and we will delete every server record for either ID, including the sign-in record. Without the IDs we cannot find them, because nothing on our server says who you are.
  • Export. Pro subscribers can export their history as a CSV file from Settings. That file is yours; we don't receive a copy.
  • Backup file. Anyone can make a backup file in Settings, under Your data, to move to a new phone. It is built on your phone and goes wherever you send it from the share sheet; we never receive it. Photos are left out unless you switch them on. If you include them, the file contains your body photos, so keep it somewhere you trust.
  • Backup. If Android backup is on for your phone, the app's database and its settings are included and restored when you set up a new phone. Photos are not included.

We cannot delete data from your phone remotely, and we cannot recover it for you if you lose the phone without a backup.

Permissions

The Android app asks for only what it needs. Camera and notifications are the two you are asked about. The rest are granted automatically at install.

  • Camera. To take the photos for an AI estimate. You can choose a photo from your library instead.
  • Notifications. For the weigh-in, measure and weekly read reminders you switch on. After a restart the phone uses the reboot permission to set them up again.
  • Internet and network state. To reach our server and to see whether you are online.
  • Billing. So Google Play can handle a Pro subscription.
  • Advertising ID. Used by Google AdMob for the banner ads shown to Free users, as described below.
  • Vibrate, keep awake, storage on older Android. Standard permissions that come with the app's libraries: button feedback, finishing a task such as an ad or a reminder, and saving your own export or backup file on Android 12 and earlier.

The app does not ask for your location, contacts, microphone, calendar or phone.

Ads, children, changes and contact

Ads. Free users see banner ads served by Google AdMob. The app asks AdMob for non-personalised ads only, so ads are not chosen from a profile of you. Google may still use your device's advertising ID, IP address and similar information to limit how often an ad is shown, to measure ads and to prevent fraud, as described in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services. You can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings.

Pro subscribers see no ads, and the app never asks AdMob for an ad for them. The AdMob library is still built into the app for everyone, though, and Android can start parts of it when the app opens. So Google may receive your advertising ID, IP address and basic device information even if you are a subscriber. We cannot switch that off for some users and not others.

Children. The app is for adults. It is not directed at anyone under 18, and the AI estimate refuses photos that appear to show a minor. If you believe a child has used the app, contact us and we will help remove any server-side records.

Security. All traffic between the app and our server is encrypted. Our server runs on Supabase, which hosts the anonymous sign-in records, the AI usage and request logs, promo codes and the AI relay. No photos or measurements are stored there.

Changes. If this policy changes in a way that affects you, the app will tell you the next time you open it. The date at the top of this page is always the current version.

Reporting AI output. Every AI photo estimate and weekly read has a Report this link. It opens your own email app with a draft addressed to us, already holding what the AI wrote, its date, the prompt version and the app version. The app sends nothing itself. If you send the email, we receive what is in it, including your email address, and use it only to look into the report and improve the AI. Ask and we will delete it.

Promo codes. Some codes, such as tester and giveaway codes, work on one phone at a time. For those, our server keeps your phone's device ID (the same app-only ID used to count the free estimate, not your advertising ID) next to your anonymous ID, so it can tell your phone from someone else's. If the code moves to a new phone, the old phone's access ends and the record points at the new one. Thirty days after a code stops working, the device ID is removed from it. Review codes for app reviewers keep no device ID.

Feedback. Send feedback in Settings opens your email app with the app version, the Android version and your anonymous ID filled in. Nothing is sent unless you send it. If you do, we receive what is in the email, including your email address, and use it only to answer you and fix the app. If a screen in LEANR crashes, it offers Email this to us, which opens your email app the same way with the error message, technical details of where in the app it happened, and the app and Android versions. It does not add your measurements or any ID, and nothing is sent unless you send it. LEANR has no crash reporter of its own.
